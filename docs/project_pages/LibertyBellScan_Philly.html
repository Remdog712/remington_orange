Liberty Bell Digital Twin

Philadelphia capture, structured-light scanning, and post-processing

View Content Page Visit VAR Project Page View Sketchfab Model

The larger project grew out of VAR Lab's December 2025 Liberty Bell replica scan at Penn State Behrend, and from May 25 to May 27, 2026, I worked with the team in Philadelphia to document the Liberty Bell and its surrounding exhibit environment during tightly scheduled before-hours and after-hours museum access windows. The public-facing project later launched on the VAR Lab Liberty Bell page, where the team describes the effort as a free, high-resolution digital twin built for preservation, research, education, and accessibility.

My role centered on the structured-light scanning workflow and much of the post-processing that followed. On site, I focused heavily on scanning the bell with the Artec Space Spider II and Artec Eva, using the higher-detail Spider workflow for close surface work and the Eva for broader coverage and faster capture. That meant planning passes around the bell's inscriptions, crack, contours, crown, and hard-to-reach geometry while checking coverage in real time on a Lenovo Legion processing system.

After capture, I handled a large share of the post-processing pipeline by reviewing scan passes, organizing the data, aligning overlapping captures, removing weak or redundant information, refining the mesh, and preparing clean outputs for textured presentation. In software, that workflow moved through Artec Studio for scan alignment, fusion, cleanup, and texture-oriented preparation, then into Blender for presentation-ready views and later media development. A big part of the job was making sure the final bell asset still felt like the real object, preserving the aged bronze tones, inscriptions, worn surface character, and the historically important crack rather than over-smoothing them away.

The larger Liberty Bell project used multiple capture systems because, as the VAR page explains, no single tool could document everything. Alongside the Artec structured-light workflow, the team also used a FARO Quantum X FaroArm with FARO Blu xS and FARO Blu xR laser-line probes for high-accuracy geometric documentation, a Matterport Pro3 for virtual tours of the Liberty Bell Center, a Canon EOS R5 Mark II for photography, video, and photogrammetry, and QooCam, Insta360 Pro 2, and Insta360 X5 cameras for immersive media capture. Together, that stack supported the public Sketchfab model, technical reference outputs, site documentation, and the broader media campaign built around the scan.

Role

Structured-light scan lead and post-processing contributor for the Liberty Bell's textured digital twin and downstream visualization assets.

Primary Workflow

Artec Space Spider II and Artec Eva capture, live QC on Lenovo Legion hardware, Artec Studio cleanup and fusion, then Blender-ready output prep.

Project Stack

FARO Quantum X with Blu xS and Blu xR probes, Matterport Pro3, Canon EOS R5 Mark II, QooCam, Insta360 Pro 2, Insta360 X5, Artec Studio, and Blender.
